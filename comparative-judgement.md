# Comparative Judgement

Exploration cluster on judging pupils' work by comparing pairs of scripts instead of marking each against a mark scheme. It started as reading in spring 2026 and became practical when the AI route for mock feedback was blocked.

## Why I am looking at it

Our moderation problem is concentrated in one place: the 16-mark "how far do you agree" question, where the department was 2.1 marks generous at the [[mock-moderation-2025-12-10|December 2025 moderation]]. Levels marking asks each marker to hold the whole mark scheme in mind; comparative judgement only asks which of two essays is better. Judges tend to agree on that more readily than on a mark.

## What I have read

- [[law-of-comparative-judgment]]: the Wikipedia article on Thurstone's model, the background to pairwise judging.
- [[kinnear-2025-comparative-judgement-reliability]]: meta-analysis of 101 datasets; about ten comparisons per script are enough, and a reliability threshold of 0.8 rather than 0.7 is safer.

## What I plan to do

- Trial on the November 2026 mocks, 16-mark question only: [[comparative-judgement-trial-plan]].
- Open question on whether the result could ever replace levels marking for mock grades: [[is-comparative-judgement-reliable-enough-for-mock-grades]].

## Limits I already see

- It ranks; it does not grade. We would still need anchor scripts from Merrow exemplars to turn ranks into grades.
- It gives no feedback by itself. It would sit alongside [[whole-class-feedback]], not replace it.
- Three judges (me, Ruth, Tomasz) and 28 scripts means about 140 judgements in total for ten per script, roughly 47 each. Manageable once, not every fortnight.

If the trial works, this cluster probably moves under the [[department-moderation-protocol]] rather than staying exploratory.
