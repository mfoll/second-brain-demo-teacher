# Whole-Class Feedback Trial

One term of replacing written comments in Year 9 books with whole-class feedback sheets, timed so the department could decide on evidence rather than preference. Completed; adopted from September 2025.

## Outcome

Written comments in Year 9 books replaced by whole-class feedback sheets for one term; marking time measured; adopted by the department from September 2025.

## Current State

- Ran with my two Year 9 classes in the summer term 2025, 22 April to 18 July.
- Marking time per set of about 30 books fell from a median of 2 hours 40 minutes to 55 minutes ([[whole-class-feedback-marking-time-log]]).
- Book scrutiny in June found redrafted paragraphs in 26 of 30 sampled books against 9 of 30 in the spring term, when pupils had individual comments.
- Pupil survey: 61% preferred the sheet, 24% preferred comments, the rest did not mind ([[pupil-survey-whole-class-feedback-2025]]).
- Agreed with Ruth on 14 July ([[whole-class-feedback-trial-review-2025-07-14]]) and adopted for Years 7-9 from September 2025. Year 11 essays keep a short individual comment.

## Scope and Boundaries

Key Stage 3 books. Exam-class essays were deliberately excluded: there was no agreement to risk them in a trial term.

## Working Context

The sheet has four boxes: what went well across the class, common misconceptions, literacy errors to fix, and a redraft task. Pupils spend the first 20 minutes of the next lesson on the redraft. The template is [[whole-class-feedback-sheet-template]]; the first version, which had a fifth "praise" box nobody used, is archived as [[whole-class-feedback-sheet-v1]].

Desmond's worry was that pupils who need individual attention would get less. The answer we settled on: I note three or four books per set that need a conversation, and have it during the redraft time. That is in [[run-whole-class-feedback]].

## Next Actions

None. Routine practice now.
