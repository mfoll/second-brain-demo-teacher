# Second-Order Concepts

The ideas historians use to organise and argue about the past, as opposed to the content of the past itself. The list we use: causation, consequence, change and continuity, similarity and difference, significance, evidence and interpretations. Chronology sits beneath all of them.

## How we use them

- Each Key Stage 3 enquiry targets one ([[enquiry-question]]). Across the 15 units each concept appears at least twice, so pupils meet it again with more knowledge behind it.
- The four-point descriptors on assessed pieces describe progress in that concept, not in "history" in general ([[ks3-assessment-model]]).
- At Key Stage 4 the Merrow question stems map onto them: "explain why" is causation, "how far do you agree" usually change or significance.

## Caveat

The concepts are only as good as the knowledge pupils have to apply them to. A pupil who knows little about the Black Death cannot argue about how far it changed things however well taught the concept is. That is the point of [[substantive-and-disciplinary-knowledge]].
