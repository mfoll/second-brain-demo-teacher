# Diverse Curriculum Audit

An audit of whose stories the old Years 7 to 9 schemes told, done with [[priyanka-sethi]] in spring 2025 as the first step of the [[ks3-curriculum-redesign]].

## Outcome

Audit of whose stories the old Years 7-9 schemes told, with 9 recommended changes accepted by the department.

## Current State

- Completed 23 May 2025. All nine recommendations were accepted on 21 May ([[diverse-curriculum-audit-presentation-2025-05-21]]).
- Seven are in the new curriculum; two depend on resources still being written (the Year 9 ropeworks enquiry and a Year 8 unit on migration to the town).

## Working Context

Method: every lesson in the 2019 schemes coded for the people pupils meet as named individuals or groups, by gender, social class, where they came from, and whether they act or are acted upon. Coded in a spreadsheet ([[diverse-curriculum-audit-spreadsheet]]) across 168 lessons. Priyanka and I coded independently and then compared the first 20 lessons; agreement was about 85%, so we split the rest.

Headline findings:

- 71% of named individuals were rulers, generals or politicians.
- Women were named in 9% of lessons, mostly queens.
- Working people appeared mainly as crowds, casualties or statistics.
- Nothing local at all, despite a town with a well-documented industrial history.
- People from beyond the country appeared almost only as subjects of empire, never in their own right.

The nine recommendations:

1. Every enquiry names at least three ordinary people with their own words where sources allow.
2. A local enquiry in Year 9 ([[local-history-enquiry]]).
3. A Year 8 enquiry on who came to Corrandale and why.
4. Medieval units use at least one source from outside the kingdom.
5. Empire taught from more than one viewpoint, with sources from the colonised.
6. Women's work as a thread in the industrial units.
7. Source packs reviewed by [[bethan-lloyd-hughes]] for range as well as reading level.
8. Re-audit after one year of each new year's units.
9. No change made only for representation if it costs the enquiry its question.

Number 9 was Desmond's addition and the department agreed with it.
