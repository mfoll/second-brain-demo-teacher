# Substantive And Disciplinary Knowledge

The distinction between what pupils know about the past (substantive knowledge: events, people, places, and concepts like "monarchy" or "empire") and what they know about how history is made and argued (disciplinary knowledge: the [[second-order-concepts]], how sources and interpretations work).

## Why it matters for us

The old 2019 schemes were heavy on substantive knowledge and light on disciplinary. The risk with an enquiry curriculum is the reverse: lots of practice at arguing, not enough to argue about. Each enquiry booklet therefore lists the substantive knowledge pupils must secure, and the retrieval quizzes test that list ([[retrieval-practice]]).

Some substantive concepts recur across years: "monarchy", "parliament", "empire", "trade", "industry", "migration". We track where each appears in [[ks3-curriculum-map-2026-27]], so a Year 9 enquiry on empire can lean on Year 8's.

## In practice

- Knowledge organiser at the front of each booklet: substantive only.
- "Thinking like a historian" box at the end of each lesson: disciplinary only.
- Desmond's chronology objection is really a substantive knowledge objection: pupils lack a connected narrative ([[chronological-framework]]).
