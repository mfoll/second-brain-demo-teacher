# Local History Enquiry

The Year 9 enquiry "Why did the ropeworks close?", built on consented clips from the [[ropeworks-voices]] interviews, the archive's company records and the Courier's coverage. Recommendation 2 of the [[diverse-curriculum-audit]]. Planned with [[luis-ortega]] in geography, who teaches the town's land use in Year 9 the same term.

## Outcome

Year 9 enquiry "Why did the ropeworks close?" using consented oral history clips, first taught in the spring term 2027.

- Clip shortlist: 8 of a target 10 chosen with Luis in October ([[clip-selection-for-year-9-enquiry]]), each checked against the society's [[ropeworks-interview-log]] before it goes into a booklet or on a slide.

## Scope and Boundaries

The enquiry, its booklet, clip set and assessment. The interviews, consent and deposit belong to the society project ([[ropeworks-voices]]). No clip is used outside the classroom: not on Quillyard pages visible to parents, not on the school website. Pupils hear clips in lessons only.

## Working Context

The enquiry asks pupils to weigh four causes ([[corrandale-ropeworks]]): falling demand for natural-fibre rope, cheaper imported synthetic twine, the parent group's decisions after 1981, and local choices such as the 1984 machinery investment. Interview clips give the workers' view; the company records in [[corrandale-archives-ropeworks-catalogue]] and the [[courier-ropeworks-closure-1986|Courier coverage]] give the official one. The point is the disagreement between them.

Why so strict on consent: the interviewees agreed to specific uses. The 2019 form offered only archive deposit. Using those voices in a classroom of 30 teenagers who may know their grandchildren is not something they agreed to, however harmless it seems. [[maureen-castleford]] and I agreed this in October 2025, and it is the rule in [[conduct-an-oral-history-interview]].
