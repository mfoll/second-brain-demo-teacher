# Comparative judgement as a research tool: A meta-analysis of application and reliability.

Kinnear G, Jones I, Davies B. *Behavior Research Methods*, 2025.

## Summary

An analysis of 101 comparative judgement datasets from several disciplines, aimed at the practical questions anyone running CJ faces: how many judges, how many comparisons, and how to judge reliability. Two main findings: about ten comparisons per item is generally enough, which is more lenient than earlier guidance; and scale separation reliability can stand in for inter-rater reliability, but the threshold should be 0.8 rather than the usual 0.7.

## Why It Matters

This is the paper that turned the [[comparative-judgement]] reading into a plan I could cost. With 28 scripts and ten comparisons each, the trial needs 140 judgements in total, shared between three of us ([[comparative-judgement-trial-plan]]). The 0.8 threshold is my success criterion in [[is-comparative-judgement-reliable-enough-for-mock-grades]]. Found on 2 October 2026, two days after the DPO said no to AI tools.

## Caveats

A research-methods paper, not about school marking as such; some datasets are not essays. Ten comparisons per item is a typical figure, not a guarantee for our scripts.
