# Oral History in Evaluation: A New Partnership to Expand and Enhance Both Fields

Sharp SR, Lin JCP, Kwan PP, Mason S, Wilkerson J, Grayson A. The Oral History Review, 2024.

## Summary

The authors used oral histories to evaluate an undergraduate research and mentoring programme and report what they learned about combining the two fields. They found little prior literature on oral history as an evaluation method, and describe its benefits for understanding participants' lived experience, its limitations, and how the method can be adapted to meet evaluation needs while respecting narrators.

## Why It Matters

Hollis Granger recommended it in July 2025 while Ewan was rethinking the consent form. Its discussion of narrators' experience of being interviewed and how their accounts are used supported the case for [[layered-consent]] and the [[shared-authority]] practice of reading back summaries. It also gave Ewan the idea, not yet acted on, of short interviews with tutors as part of the evidence in the [[mlp-attendance-project-write-up]].

## Caveats

A methods reflection from one programme evaluation, not a study of community oral history projects.
