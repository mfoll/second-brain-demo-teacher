# Year 7 Enquiry Units

Writing the five Year 7 enquiries over the summer term and holidays of 2025, so that Year 7 could start the new curriculum in September 2025, a year ahead of Years 8 and 9.

## Outcome

Five Year 7 enquiries with booklets, retrieval quizzes and one assessed piece each, taught from September 2025.

## Current State

- Completed 1 September 2025; taught through 2025-26 and again from September 2026.
- After the first year, booklets 2 and 4 were rewritten for reading level and the assessed piece in enquiry 3 was replaced ([[history-department-meeting-2026-07-08]]).

## Working Context

The five enquiries, in teaching order:

1. Why did William win in 1066? (causation; opens after the two timeline lessons)
2. How did the Normans keep control? (change and continuity)
3. How far did the Black Death change life for ordinary people? (consequence; uses [[black-death-wikipedia]] as teacher background)
4. What can a medieval town's records tell us? (evidence)
5. Was the break with Rome a revolution? (significance)

Each has a booklet of about 24 pages ([[year-7-enquiry-booklets]]), six to eight retrieval quizzes built in [[quillyard]] by reusing the format of the [[retrieval-quiz-bank]], and one assessed extended answer marked with a four-point descriptor.

Writing split: I wrote 1, 3 and 5; Priyanka 2 and 4; [[bethan-lloyd-hughes]] built the source packs; [[ruth-calder]] read everything once. About 70 hours of my time across May to August, roughly half of it in the summer holiday. That number fed my view on how long the migration booklets would take, which turned out optimistic anyway ([[migration-booklet-check-in-2026-05-06]]).

## Lessons kept

- Write the [[enquiry-question]] and the final assessed task first, then the lessons backwards from them ([[plan-an-enquiry-unit]]).
- 24 pages is the upper limit; enquiry 3 at 31 pages was not finished by two classes.
- Teachers want a one-page teacher overview per enquiry more than full lesson plans.
