# Ropeworks Themes So Far

Ewan's working list of themes across the 19 Ropeworks Voices interviews, updated 19 October 2026. Drawn from the one-page summaries, not transcripts. For use in the anniversary talk and for clip selection.

## Themes

1. **The walk.** Every ropewalk worker describes its length, the noise and walking miles a day. Strong sensory material.
2. **Two works under one roof.** Men's and women's work were separate until the 1970s synthetic lines mixed them. Women interviewees describe this more than men do.
3. **Family firm to branch plant.** After the 1971 takeover, "decisions came in a brown envelope". Almost every interviewee dates the decline from there.
4. **1984.** The new spinning machines. Ropewalk men mostly see them as window dressing; spinners mostly see a real attempt to save the works. The sharpest disagreement in the collection.
5. **Short time, 1985.** Three-day weeks, rumours, the queue at the wages office.
6. **After.** Where people went: the new distribution depot, the council, early retirement, leaving town. Several say the town "lost its clock" because the works hooter stopped.

## Gaps

Maintenance fitters (1 interview), office and management (3), anyone who started after 1980 (none). RV20 to RV22 should help.

## Caution

These are patterns across summaries, not findings. Seven of the 19 cannot yet be quoted in school; see [[can-2019-form-ropeworks-interviews-be-used-in-lessons]].
