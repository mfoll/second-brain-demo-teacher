# Primary source

English Wikipedia article.

## Summary

Defines a primary source in historical study as an artefact, document, recording or similar created at the time under study, and notes that library science and journalism use related but different definitions; in journalism a primary source can be a person with direct knowledge.

## Why It Matters

Used to write the 'What is a source?' page in Year 7 enquiry 4 ([[year-7-enquiry-units]]). The journalism definition is the one I grew up with at the Courier, and comparing the two is a good way into the oral history clips in Year 9: an interviewee is a primary source about their own experience, not about the company's decisions ([[local-history-enquiry]]).

## Caveats

The primary/secondary distinction is less tidy than the article's lead suggests; we teach it as a question of what the source is evidence for.
