# How Do We Measure Attendance Impact Without a Control Group?

Year 10 attendance rose from 91.4% to 93.2% and persistently absent pupils fell from 31 to 19. How much of that was the drive?

## Why it is hard

- No comparison group: every Year 10 tutor group took part.
- Whole-school attendance also moved over the year; the size of that shift matters.
- Pupils who start very low tend to drift back towards the average anyway.
- Some pupils left or joined the year group.

## Options considered

- Compare with the previous Year 10 at the same point. Different cohort, different year; useful as context only.
- Compare with other year groups in the same year. The best available; asked Donna Whitlock for the figures.
- Compare tutor groups by how consistently they made first-day calls. Small numbers, but a dose-response pattern would be suggestive.
- Use another trust school's Year 10 doing a similar project. Two MLP colleagues agreed to share aggregates; different schools, so weak.

## Current plan

Report the year-group comparison and the tutor-group pattern, and say plainly that neither proves cause. Treat it as practitioner [[action-research-wikipedia|action research]], not an evaluation.
