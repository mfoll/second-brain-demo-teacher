# What Works to Keep Students in School? A Meta-Analysis of Interventions to Reduce School Absenteeism

Li D, Singh S, Jeldi N, Chang S, Zhu M, Dailey K, Karabukayeva A, Cai C. *Behavioral Sciences*, 2026.

## Summary

A meta-analysis of 13 randomised and quasi-experimental studies published between 2020 and early 2025, all reporting school absenteeism as an outcome, from pre-school to the end of secondary school in the United States. The pooled effect of interventions was small but statistically significant. Effects varied a lot between studies, and the only moderator that reached significance was setting: interventions run in a single school did better than those spread over several.

## Why It Matters

I read it in September 2026 for the [[mlp-attendance-project-write-up]], because [[catriona-dalgleish]] asked every participant to place their project against published evidence. It supports two things I want to say: expect small effects, and the way a school runs an approach matters at least as much as which approach it picks. That fits the [[year-10-attendance-drive]], where the routine of the weekly huddle seemed to matter more than any single tactic ([[the-weekly-huddle-matters-more-than-the-letters]]).

## Caveats

- US studies only, in a different system from ours.
- Thirteen studies with high heterogeneity; the moderator analysis has little power.
- It says nothing specific about first-day calls, so it does not answer [[does-first-day-calling-change-attendance]].
