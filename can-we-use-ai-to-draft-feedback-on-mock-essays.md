# Can We Use AI To Draft Feedback On Mock Essays?

Can I use an AI tool to draft whole-class feedback on the Year 11 mock essays, starting with the November 2026 mocks, without breaking data protection rules or weakening the feedback?

## Current Answer

> [!info]
> Not yet. On 30 September 2026 the trust data protection officer, Paul Ingram, said no pupil work may go into an AI tool the trust has not approved until the trust's data protection impact assessment is finished, expected December 2026. For the November mocks I mark by hand and trial comparative judgement on the 16-mark question instead.

## Why It Matters

Marking 28 mock scripts properly takes me about 11 hours, of which the 16-mark essays take six. The part I hoped to speed up is not the mark but the whole-class feedback sheet: spotting the misconceptions and structural errors common across the set. If a tool could draft that in an hour and I corrected it, the saving would be real and the marking would stay mine. My stance is in [[ai-feedback-is-a-workload-tool-not-a-marking-tool]].

## What we know

- **Data protection.** Mock scripts are pupil personal data. Anonymising does not help, because essays can identify a pupil by content and handwriting; Paul said so explicitly ([[dpo-ai-tools-decision-2026-09-30]]). The trust's [[data-protection-impact-assessment]] covers which tools, what data, where it is processed and how long it is kept.
- **Trust guidance.** The [[trust-interim-ai-guidance-2026]] allows staff to use approved tools for planning with no pupil data, and nothing else.
- **Quality.** The research on AI essay scoring is about scoring, not feedback, and about large datasets, not 28 scripts. [[debelak-2026-ai-essay-scoring-testing-standards]] sets out how validity, fairness and reliability should be tested; nobody has done that for our mark scheme. [[atkinson-2025-llm-hybrid-essay-scoring]] reports strong agreement on public essay datasets, which says little about a history argument. Options compared in [[ai-feedback-options-comparison]].
- **Interim alternative.** [[comparative-judgement]] addresses the other problem, consistency on the 16-mark question, without any pupil data leaving the school. Plan in [[comparative-judgement-trial-plan]].

## What would answer it

1. The trust DPIA concludes and names approved tools and conditions (December 2026).
2. If a tool is approved: a small check on the March 2027 mocks. I write my own whole-class sheet first, then compare the tool's draft against it for errors and omissions on 10 scripts.
3. Ruth agrees, as head of department, that it fits department marking policy.

## Rejected options

- Typing anonymised extracts into a personal account of a public chatbot. Ruled out by Paul and by me before I asked him.
- Waiting for [[quillyard]] to add an AI feature. Nothing has been announced with a date.
