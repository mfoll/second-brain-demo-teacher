# Blind Double-Marking

A second marker marks a script without seeing the first marker's marks or comments, and the two are compared afterwards. It measures agreement rather than checking whether the second marker can be persuaded.

The department's version, from the [[department-moderation-protocol]], double-marks 10% of mock scripts, chosen across the mark range, after both markers have standardised on board exemplars. A difference of more than two marks on a 16-mark answer goes to a third reader. In March 2026 the average difference on the 16-mark question was 0.6 marks ([[march-2026-mock-results-summary]]).

It costs about 40 minutes per teacher per round. Copies are made with marks covered; doing it on the original scripts with sticky notes did not work because the notes were readable through the paper.
