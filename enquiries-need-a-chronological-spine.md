# Enquiries Need A Chronological Spine

## Stance

Desmond is more right than the department has admitted. Enquiries are the best way I know to teach pupils to argue, but on their own they do not give pupils a sense of the long run of the past. The timeline lessons are a start, not a solution.

## Scope

Key Stage 3, especially the transition from Year 9 to the thematic study in Year 10, which assumes a thousand-year framework ([[thematic-study]]).

## Why I think this

- Desmond's July 2026 check: Year 7 could order their five enquiries but not place them in a wider frame ([[history-department-meeting-2026-07-08]]).
- My 10H1 in September 2026 struggled to put the Norman Conquest, the Reformation and industrialisation in order on the first migration lesson, after three years of enquiries (the old schemes for them, admittedly).
- As a reporter I learned that people remember stories, not lists. A cumulative timeline is a list. A spine needs a story told quickly: an overview lesson every half-term that retells the whole sweep in ten minutes.

## What Would Change My Mind

If the January 2027 Year 8 chronology test ([[is-a-cumulative-timeline-enough-for-chronology]]) shows most pupils can place events across all of Key Stage 3 reliably, the timeline is doing its job and I am wrong.

## Implications

- I add a five-minute "where are we?" overview at the start of every enquiry in my own classes.
- I will propose half-termly overview lessons at the July 2027 review if the test results are poor.
