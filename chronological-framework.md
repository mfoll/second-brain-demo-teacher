# Chronological Framework

A pupil's mental map of the past in order: the big periods, roughly when they were, and where particular events sit within them. Not the same as knowing dates, and not the same as being taught in chronological order.

## Why it is contested here

[[desmond-hartigan]]'s objection at the [[ks3-curriculum-review-2025-06-25|curriculum review of June 2025]] was that enquiries break the past into six-week islands, so pupils never build the framework. The department's answer:

- Two timeline lessons at the start of each year ([[ks3-timeline-lessons-outline]]).
- A cumulative timeline in the back of every exercise book, added to at the end of each enquiry ([[cumulative-timeline-template]]).
- At least two chronology questions in every retrieval quiz.
- Enquiries in chronological order within each year.

## What we know so far

Desmond's informal check in July 2026: Year 7 could order their own five enquiries but could not place them against anything outside the course ([[history-department-meeting-2026-07-08]]). That fits the idea that a framework needs more than the events taught: it needs the gaps between them sketched in. Testing properly in January 2027 ([[is-a-cumulative-timeline-enough-for-chronology]]).
