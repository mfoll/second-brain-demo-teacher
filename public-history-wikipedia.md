# Public history

English Wikipedia article.

## Summary

Describes public history as the work of people trained in history who practise mostly outside academic settings, rooted in historic preservation, archives, oral history and museum work. Lists typical settings such as museums, historic sites, archives, broadcasting, new media and government, and notes the field's professionalisation since the late 1970s.

## Why It Matters

Read after the Esterbrook seminar to understand [[hollis-granger]]'s field. It recasts the society's [[ropeworks-voices]] project as public history: historians working with a community on its own past. That framing is useful for the final lesson of the Year 9 enquiry and for [[how-does-a-town-remember-an-industry-it-lost]].

## Caveats

The article's examples are mostly North American institutions; the volunteer-society end of public history gets little space.
