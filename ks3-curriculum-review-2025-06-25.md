# KS3 Curriculum Review 2025-06-25

Department review of the draft Key Stage 3 curriculum before the Year 7 units went to print. The meeting where Desmond Hartigan made the chronology objection, and where the timeline lessons came from.

## At a Glance

> [!note]
> Desmond argued that stand-alone enquiries leave pupils without a sense of chronology. Compromise: every year group starts with two timeline lessons, and pupils keep a cumulative timeline in the back of their books, adding each enquiry as they go. Desmond accepted it; he still thinks it is not enough.

## Who and where

Wednesday 25 June 2025, 15:30 to 17:45, history office. [[ruth-calder]], [[priyanka-sethi]] (presenting), [[desmond-hartigan]], [[tomasz-zielinski]], [[amelia-fairweather]], me.

## The draft

Priyanka presented the 15-enquiry map: five a year, each about six weeks, each with one second-order concept and one assessed piece. Year 7 booklets were drafted; Years 8 and 9 outlined. The [[diverse-curriculum-audit]] recommendations were mapped onto units.

## The objection

Desmond's case, close to how he put it: in 31 years he has seen enquiry curricula produce Year 9s who can write a balanced paragraph about the causes of something but cannot say whether it came before or after the Reformation. Each enquiry is a six-week island. Pupils need a narrative frame to hang them on, and that frame used to come from teaching in order.

Points made in reply:

- Ruth: the enquiries are in chronological order within each year; the order is still there.
- Priyanka: the old schemes were chronological and Year 9 still could not place events; the problem is retrieval, not order.
- Me: both can be true. Within-year order does not give pupils the long view across three years.
- Tomasz: his own school had a timeline on the classroom wall that nobody looked at.

## Options

1. **Leave as drafted.** Rejected: Desmond's point was taken seriously by everyone.
2. **Return to a chronological survey with enquiry questions added.** Rejected: that is what the textbooks did ([[textbook-procurement]]).
3. **A chronology unit at the start of Year 7 only.** Rejected: one unit does not hold for three years.
4. **Two timeline lessons at the start of every year, plus a cumulative timeline pupils keep in their books.** Proposed by me, refined by Priyanka. Agreed.

## Decision

Option 4. Each year opens with two timeline lessons: the first reviews the long sweep (Year 7 from c.1000 to the present in broad periods; Years 8 and 9 recap what they have done and preview the year), the second places the year's five enquiries. Each enquiry ends with pupils adding its key dates to the cumulative timeline. Retrieval quizzes include at least two chronology questions each.

Desmond accepted the compromise "for now", with a review after a full year of Year 7 teaching. At that review in July 2026 ([[history-department-meeting-2026-07-08]]) he said the timeline helped but was not enough, and he still says so ([[is-a-cumulative-timeline-enough-for-chronology]]).
