# Shared Authority

The idea, common in oral and public history, that an interview's meaning is made jointly by the narrator and the historian, so the narrator keeps a real say in how their account is used and interpreted.

## In Ropeworks Voices

- Interviewees hear a summary of what they said before they sign, and choose its uses.
- Summaries describe what the interviewee said in their terms, not ours. "She thinks the 1984 machines were a real attempt to save the works", not "she is mistaken about 1984".
- Two interviewees have been sent the clips proposed for school use and asked if they are happy with the cut.

## For the Year 9 enquiry

Pupils will be told that the workers disagree with each other and with the company records, and that the disagreement is the evidence. The voices are not there to illustrate a textbook answer.

## Tension

Shared authority takes time. A teacher with 15 enquiries a year cannot send every clip back to its narrator. For the school version the rule is simpler: use only whole sentences, never splice, and always give the provenance line.
