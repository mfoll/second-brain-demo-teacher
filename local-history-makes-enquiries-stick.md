# Local History Makes Enquiries Stick

## Stance

A local enquiry, using voices pupils might recognise, will be remembered longer than any other Key Stage 3 unit. It is also the unit most likely to go wrong if consent and sensitivity are handled carelessly.

## Scope

The Year 9 ropeworks enquiry; possibly a Year 8 unit on migration to the town.

## Why I think this

- Pupils ask about the ropeworks without prompting when they learn I recorded interviews. Several have grandparents who worked there.
- At the Esterbrook seminar ([[esterbrook-public-history-seminar-2026-03-18]]) teachers who had run local oral history units described the same effect.
- Research on collective memory suggests people recall local figures and events better than distant ones ([[macedo-2026-local-premium-in-collective-memory]]), though that is about adults and online attention, not lessons.

## What Would Change My Mind

The retention check after the first teaching in spring 2027. If Year 9 remember it no better than other enquiries a term later, it is a nice unit, not a sticky one.

## Implications

Confidence is low because it has not been taught yet. Do not use this argument to justify more local units until there is evidence.
