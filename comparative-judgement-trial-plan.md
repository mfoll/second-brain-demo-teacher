# Comparative Judgement Trial Plan

Plan for a comparative judgement round on the 16-mark Paper 1 question of the November 2026 mocks, 11H1 only. Agreed with Ruth on 7 October 2026 as the interim alternative to AI-drafted feedback.

## Aim

Find out whether pairwise judging gives a reliable rank order for the 16-mark answers and whether it agrees with our levels marks after standardisation. It is a check on marking, not a replacement, this round.

## Method

1. Scan the 28 answers to the 16-mark question; cover names with candidate numbers. Scans stay on the school network.
2. Judges: me, [[ruth-calder]], [[tomasz-zielinski]]. About 140 judgements in total, so each script appears in about ten pairs; roughly 47 judgements each.
3. Pairs drawn by a short spreadsheet script so each script appears about ten times and no pair repeats.
4. Judges answer one question per pair: which is the better answer to the question set? One line of reason when it is hard to call.
5. Fit the scale (Bradley-Terry in the spreadsheet add-on), report scale separation reliability.
6. Compare the rank with our moderated levels marks.

## Success criteria

- Reliability of 0.8 or more.
- No script more than one level away from its levels mark without a reason we can name.

## Timing

Judging 28 November to 4 December, alongside levels marking. Practice run done on 21 October ([[comparative-judgement-practice-round-2026-10-21]]).

## Not doing

No grades from the rank this round. No pupil data outside the school network, which is why the online CJ services we looked at are out until the DPIA too.
