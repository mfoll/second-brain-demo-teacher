# Pilot Before A Whole-Department Switch

## Stance

A department should not switch everyone to a new exam option at once. Pilot it with the people who wrote the materials, then switch the rest when the materials and the evidence exist.

## Scope

Key Stage 4 option changes and any large curriculum change where results are at stake. Less true at Key Stage 3, where nobody's grade depends on one year.

## Why I think this

- The February 2026 vote was made on enthusiasm and an estimate. My estimate of 75 hours for five booklets was wrong: we had spent 62 hours for 40% of the work by May ([[migration-booklet-check-in-2026-05-06]]).
- We voted before the board's specimen papers existed. They changed what the booklets needed.
- A whole-department switch puts new content on the least experienced teacher at the same time as everyone else. A pilot keeps it with the authors.
- A pilot gives you a comparison. A full switch gives you only next year's results and an argument about cohorts.

## What Would Change My Mind

If the specification, specimen materials and complete resources were all available at the time of the vote, a full switch would be fine.

## Implications

- When I lead something similar, I will ask for the materials and a pilot plan before the vote.
- Wrote this into my Middle Leadership Programme reflection as the main thing I got wrong this year.
