# Why Did Year 10 Attendance Stall In The Spring Term?

## Why It Matters

Year 10 attendance rose from 91.4% to 92.6% between October and February, then barely moved until the last six weeks of the summer term. The [[mlp-attendance-project-write-up]] has to explain the plateau honestly, and the 2026-27 huddle should not repeat it.

## Candidate Explanations

1. **Winter illness.** Whole-school attendance also dipped in January and February. Probably part of it; Marek's comparison with Years 9 and 11 shows a dip of similar size.
2. **First-day calls slipped.** Completion dropped from about 92% before Christmas to 81% in January ([[first-day-response]]). Re-launched on 23 February.
3. **The remaining caseload was harder.** By January the drifting pupils had mostly improved; the persistent group left were pupils with long-term difficulties, where calls and letters do little. Consistent with [[gonzalvez-2026-school-absenteeism-profiles]], which separates truancy from school refusal profiles.
4. **Letters backfired.** Stage 2 letters in January coincided with two complaints and no improvement in the 14 cases. Dropped.

## Current Answer

Probably 1 and 3 together, with 2 making it worse. I cannot separate them with the data we have: no comparison group and only one cohort.

## What Would Settle It

Running the same huddle in 2026-27 with first-day calls kept above 90% all year. If the spring still flattens, illness and caseload are the likelier explanation.
