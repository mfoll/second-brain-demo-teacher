# History Department Meeting 2026-07-08

End-of-year review of the first year of Year 7 enquiries, and final checks before Years 8 and 9 start the new curriculum in September.

## Year 7 review

- Completion: all five enquiries finished by all eight Year 7 classes; enquiry 3 overran by two lessons in three classes.
- Assessed pieces: 64% secure or above across the year on the four-point descriptor, rising from 52% on enquiry 1 to 71% on enquiry 5.
- Pupil voice ([[ks3-pupil-voice-survey-2026-07]]): booklets rated highly; timeline lessons "boring but useful" was the most common free-text comment.
- Rewrites agreed: booklets 2 and 4 for reading level (Bethan's check), enquiry 3's assessed piece replaced.

## Chronology

[[desmond-hartigan]] reported a quick check with his Year 7 class: most could order the five enquiries correctly, few could place any of them against events outside the course. His view: the timeline helps within a year but does not give a long view. Agreed to test it properly with the current Year 7 (then Year 8) in January 2027 ([[is-a-cumulative-timeline-enough-for-chronology]]).

## Migration pilot

Ruth and I confirmed booklets 1 and 2 are final; booklet 3 for October half-term ([[migration-resources-tracker]]).

## Decisions

- Years 8 and 9 start the new enquiries in September as planned.
- Retention check in January 2027 ([[how-much-do-pupils-remember-of-enquiries-a-year-later]]).
