# Corrandale Ropeworks

The rope and twine works that was Corrandale's largest employer from 1868 until it closed in 1986. It is the shared subject of the society's [[ropeworks-voices]] interviews and the Year 9 enquiry "Why did the ropeworks close?" ([[local-history-enquiry]]).

## What it was

- Founded in 1868 beside the canal basin, on a site that grew to about 11 acres. The covered ropewalk was 400 yards long, the longest building in the town until it was demolished in 1991.
- Made hemp and later manila rope for ships and mines, binder and baler twine for farms, and from the early 1960s synthetic rope and twine in polypropylene and nylon.
- Peak workforce about 1,350 in 1951. About 480 worked there at closure.
- Work was split by gender for most of its life: men in the ropewalk, maintenance and the yard; women in spinning, twine finishing and the offices. Several interviewees describe that split breaking down only in the 1970s.
- Bought by a national cordage group in 1971; decisions moved out of the town from then on.

## Why it closed

The interviews and the archive record point to several causes, which is why it works as an enquiry:

- Demand for natural-fibre rope fell as shipping and mining changed.
- Cheaper imported synthetic twine undercut the farm market through the late 1970s.
- The parent group concentrated production at newer plants after 1981.
- Short-time working in 1985, closure announced in January 1986, last shift in September 1986.

Ex-workers disagree about how much weight to give each, and about whether the 1984 investment in new spinning machines was a real attempt to save the works or cover for running it down. That disagreement is the material for the enquiry: pupils weigh testimony against the company records in [[corrandale-archives-ropeworks-catalogue]] and the Courier's coverage in [[courier-ropeworks-closure-1986]].

## Caveats

Workforce figures come from the company's own annual returns in the archive and the Courier. The 1951 peak is from a single return; the closure figure of about 480 is the Courier's, and the union's figure at the time was 510.
