# Esterbrook Public History Seminar 2026-03-18

Evening seminar at Esterbrook University, "Oral history in the classroom", led by [[hollis-granger]]. About 25 there: teachers, museum staff, two society volunteers including [[nisha-kapoor]].

## What I took away

- Let pupils hear the question as well as the answer. Testimony is produced in a conversation; cutting the interviewer out hides how.
- Pair every clip with a document that disagrees with it. Oral history is strongest when it is tested, not when it is used to "bring history to life".
- Consent is about use, not just recording. Her example of a project that had to withdraw a school resource because its forms covered archive deposit only matched our 2019 form problem exactly ([[ropeworks-consent-form-2019]]).
- Memory changes with what the town now says about the past. Interviews recorded after a closure becomes part of local identity tell you about both the closure and the identity.

## What I changed

- The ropeworks enquiry now plays the interviewer's question before each clip ([[ropeworks-enquiry-scheme-draft]]).
- Started the [[public-memory-and-heritage]] reading cluster the same week.
