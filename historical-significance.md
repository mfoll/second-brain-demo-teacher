# Historical Significance

Why something in the past matters, to whom and when. Unlike other second-order concepts, significance is openly a judgement made in the present: an event is significant because of what it changed, how many it affected, how long the effects lasted, and what it reveals or symbolises now.

## How we teach it

Year 7 enquiry 5 ("Was the break with Rome a revolution?") and the Year 9 empire enquiry use a simple set of criteria: how many people affected, how deeply, for how long, and does it still matter today. Pupils learn that two historians can apply the same criteria and disagree.

## Link to public memory

Significance and memory are close. What a town decides to remember (the ropeworks mural, the anniversary pieces in the Courier) is a public judgement of significance. The Year 9 enquiry's last lesson asks whether the town remembers the closure in proportion to what it changed ([[local-history-enquiry]], [[public-memory-and-heritage]]).
