# Semantic relatedness and the efficacy of retrieval practice.

Gupta MW, Pan SC, Rickard TC. *NPJ Science of Learning*, 2026.

## Summary

A laboratory study of when practice testing helps most. Participants learned word pairs that were either weakly or strongly related in meaning, practised them by testing with feedback or by restudy, and took a cued-recall test a day later. Testing beat restudy for both kinds of pair, but the benefit was about a quarter smaller for strongly related pairs. The authors rule out a ceiling effect and suggest that for related material, what restudy and testing strengthen overlaps more.

## Why It Matters

I noticed something like this in the [[quillyard-quiz-analytics-summer-2025]]: questions where the answer follows naturally from the question (Armada, Spain) seemed to do less than ones that demand a less obvious link (a date to an event). It nudges me to write the new migration questions for 10H1 so they ask for the less obvious connection ([[does-the-migration-pilot-need-its-own-quiz-questions]]).

## Caveats

Word pairs, adults, a 24-hour delay. Nothing about school history or months-long retention. A hint for question writing, not evidence for it.
